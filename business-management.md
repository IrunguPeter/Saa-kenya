# Saa Kenya - Business Management Guide

Practical management for a small-time watch seller in Kenya. Simple enough to
use daily, thorough enough to keep the business profitable and audit-ready.

---

## 1. The Golden Rule: Track Everything

If it isn't recorded, it didn't happen. Keep ONE master record (Excel/Google
Sheets is fine, or a notebook + receipts). Update daily, not weekly.

Recommended master sheet tabs:
- Products & Stock
- Suppliers & Purchases
- Customers
- Sales / Orders
- Expenses
- Debt/Receivables & Credits/Payables
- P&L (monthly)
- Balance sheet (monthly)
- Cash

---

## 2. Suppliers Management

Keep a supplier register with a row per supplier:

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Name | Timepiece Palace (Accra Towers) |
| Contact / WhatsApp | 07xx xxx xxx |
| Location | Nairobi CBD |
| What they supply | Cheap quartz, mix-and-match |
| Unit cost (wholesale) | KSh 150-250 |
| MOQ | 4 pieces |
| Payment terms | Cash on pickup |
| Lead time | 0 (same day) |
| Quality notes | Test leather straps, check dead units |
| Last order / date | 40 pcs, 24 Jan |
| Rating (1-5) | 4 |

Rules:
- **Always take a written/WhatsApp quote** before buying. Compare 2-3 suppliers
  per order until you know the market.
- **Sample first** (2-5 pcs) before any bulk order.
- **Negotiate per quantity** - tiered pricing (e.g. KSh 250/pc at 10, KSh 200/pc
  at 50, KSh 150/pc at 100).
- **Don't put all eggs in one basket**: keep at least 2 suppliers for the same
  product in case one runs out.
- **Defect handling**: agree in advance on return/exchange policy for dead or
  damaged units (many Chinese suppliers do 1-2 month exchanges over 20+ pcs).
- **Re-order point**: restock when stock drops below ~2 weeks of expected sales
  for fast movers, or risk "out of stock / preorder" mode on your store.

Purchase order fields to record per buy: date, supplier, items, qty, unit cost,
total, delivery/clearing cost, landed cost per unit.

## 3. Customers Management

A simple customer ledger (your store already collects name, phone, county, town
via checkout - export it):

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Name | Jane W. |
| Phone | 07xx xxx xxx |
| Channel found (referral, TikTok, WhatsApp) | Affiliate ref |
| Orders | 2 |
| Total spend | KSh 3,400 |
| Last order date | 12 Feb |
| Notes | Likes gold straps; prefers M-Pesa |

Turn customers into repeat buyers:
- **WhatsApp list** (with consent) - this is your cash machine: restock alerts,
  flash sales, new arrivals.
- **Follow up 2-5 days after delivery**: "Hi Jane, how is the watch?" - this
  also screens for quality issues early.
- **Ask for a wrist selfie / review** in exchange for a small voucher - this
  feeds your Instagram/TikTok content for free.
- **Birthday / festive offers** (Valentine's, Christmas gifting).
- **Referral loyalty**: your built-in affiliate program (10% commission, 30-day
  cookie) pays customers-turned-brand-ambassadors.
- **Segment**: students (cheap/sporty), gift buyers (couple sets), corporate
  (bulk/Casio tier).

## 4. Sales Tracking

Record every sale: date, order ref, items, qty, unit price, line total, delivery
fee, payment method (M-Pesa/card/POD), customer, source (TikTok/IG/WA/ref),
profit (see margin below).

Tier your products:
- **Heroes**: your 3-5 best sellers - keep stock, market hard.
- **Cross-sellers**: items often bought together (combo offers).
- **Dead stock**: >60 days unsold - discount to clear, don't reorder.

Key sales metrics to watch weekly:
- Units sold
- Revenue (gross sales + delivery fees)
- Average order value (AOV) = Revenue / orders
- Conversion (orders / site visitors, if your store logs visits)
- Sell-through rate = units sold / units in stock
- Best and worst days/products

## 5. Profits and Loss (P&L) Statement

The most important report. Do it monthly.

```
SAA KENYA - PROFIT & LOSS (Month)

REVENUE
  Product sales                     KSh 85,000
  Delivery fees                     KSh  3,200
  Total revenue                     KSh 88,200

COST OF GOODS SOLD (COGS)
  Opening stock (this month)        KSh 25,000
  + Purchases (this month)          KSh 40,000
  - Closing stock (end of month)    KSh 18,000
  = COGS                            KSh 47,000

GROSS PROFIT                       KSh 41,200
Gross margin % = 41,200 / 88,200   ~47%

OPERATING EXPENSES
  Delivery/logistics                KSh  6,500
  Advertising (TikTok/FB boosts)    KSh  4,000
  Affiliate commissions (10%)       KSh  2,500
  Packaging                        KSh  1,800
  M-Pesa/IntaSend fees              KSh  1,900
  Transport (your own, sourcing)    KSh  1,500
  Misc (data, phone, electricity)   KSh  1,000
  Total expenses                   KSh 19,200

NET PROFIT                         KSh 22,000
Net margin % = 22,000 / 88,200     ~25%
```

- **Gross margin** should sit 30-60% for watches at your price tier.
- **Net profit** under 10% = your pricing is wrong or costs are creeping.
- Only take "salary"/drawings OUT of net profit - don't spend from revenue.

## 6. Balance Sheet

A snapshot of what the business owns (assets), owes (liabilities), and is worth
(equity). Run it at month-end.

```
SAA KENYA - BALANCE SHEET (Month end)

ASSETS
  Cash (M-Pesa + bank + in hand)    KSh 18,000
  Stock at cost (closing stock)     KSh 18,000
  Money owed to you (if any)        KSh  2,000
  Equipment/phone used for store    KSh  5,000
  Total assets                     KSh 43,000

LIABILITIES
  Money you owe suppliers            KSh  6,000
  Unpaid affiliate payouts           KSh  2,500
  Outstanding delivery bills         KSh  1,000
  Total liabilities                KSh  9,500

EQUITY = Assets - Liabilities     KSh 33,500
```

- Equity should grow each month if you're actually profitable.
- The balance MUST balance: Assets = Liabilities + Equity.
- Use it to answer: "should I buy that bulk batch?" - only if cash/stock allows
  without breaking your buffer.

## 7. Cash Flow (the real killer of small businesses)

Profit is not cash. You can be "profitable" and broke.

Track cash in vs cash out weekly. Common cash leaks:
- Stock sitting unsold (cash trapped in inventory).
- Paying suppliers before you collect from customers.
- POD orders: money arrives after delivery - price in the delay.
- Ordering bulk on credit without a plan to sell it.

Keep a **cash buffer** (minimum 1 month's expenses) and only buy stock if you
can still cover: next month's deliveries, M-Pesa fees, and a restock.

## 8. Pricing & Margin Calculator

Work BACKWARD from target retail price.

```
Example entry watch (KSh 500 tier):
  Wholesale cost per watch          KSh 220
  + Import/clearing share           KSh  80
  = Landed cost                    KSh 300
  + Delivery (ave)                  KSh 100
  + M-Pesa fee + packaging          KSh  30
  = All-in cost                    KSh 430
  Add profit + ad allowance (aim
  gross margin ~40%)...
  Suggested retail                 KSh 500-650
```

Rule of thumb: retail = all-in cost x 2 (keeps you profitable through fees,
ads, refunds, discounts). Set your KSh 2,000 free-delivery threshold to protect
margin on small orders. Never discount below your all-in cost.

## 9. Expenses - the full list (don't forget these)

- Cost of goods (COGS)
- Delivery / courier
- Packaging (pouches, boxes, tape)
- Payment fees: M-Pesa / IntaSend (compare rates - often ~1-1.5% + flat)
- Advertising (TikTok promotions, FB boosts, influencer gifting)
- Affiliate commissions (10%)
- Transport & sourcing trips (boda, matatu)
- Storage (if any)
- Data, phone airtime, business internet
- Repairs/replacements for defective units
- Refunds & chargebacks
- KRA taxes (see below)
- Your own time/drawings (at least track it)

## 10. Taxes (KRA - Kenya)

- **Register**: KRA PIN; register as a business taxpayer. If registered for
  VAT: charge 16% VAT on VATable supplies (small traders with turnover below
  the KSh 8M/a annual threshold are often turnover-tax registered instead -
  confirm with KRA/a tax adviser).
- **Turnover tax** (TOT): for small traders with annual turnover between the
  threshold bands; typically payable monthly/quarterly on gross turnover. Check
  current KRA rates.
- **Income tax**: Nett business income taxed at your band if a sole proprietor.
- Keep every receipt and payments record - the P&L above IS your tax record.
- Set aside ~10-15% of net profit into a "tax jar" every month so tax time
  doesn't shock you. Penalties sting way more than the tax.
- Affiliate payouts and M-Pesa withdrawals are all traceable - report honestly.

## 11. Inventory Management (simplified)

- Each product line = a row: name, cost, retail, qty in, qty sold, qty
  remaining, days in stock, value.
- **Periodic counts**: weekly for heroes, monthly for everything.
- **FIFO**: sell oldest stock first to avoid dead units.
- **Slow movers**: after 60 days, discount 10% then 20%; after 90 days, bundle
  or use as ad/affiliate freebie.
- **Scrap/reject rate**: if >5% of a batch is defective, switch supplier.
- Stock is an asset on the balance sheet, not spending - but cash tied up in
  stock is cash you can't use.

## 12. Weekly & Monthly Routines (keep it light)

WEEKLY (30-45 min):
1. Update sales + stock numbers.
2. Check cash in vs cash out.
3. Review which content/platform brought the most sales; double down.
4. Restock fast movers; flag slow movers for discounting.

MONTHLY (1-2 hrs):
1. Count/live-check stock; reconcile numbers.
2. Build the P&L (Section 5).
3. Update balance sheet (Section 6).
4. Pay affiliates, clear supplier balances.
5. Set next month's budget (max ad spend, expected sales, stock buys).
6. Move ~10-15% of net profit to the tax jar; take the rest as drawings only if
   real cash exists.

## 13. KPIs to Watch (just these)

| Metric | Healthy target |
|--------|----------------|
| Gross margin | 30-60% |
| Net margin | 10%+ |
| Average order value | KSh 1,000+ |
| Sell-through / 30 days | 30%+ |
| Defect/return rate | Under 5% |
| Cash buffer | 1+ months of expenses |
| Stock days unsold | Under 60 |
| Cost per sale (ads) | Below KSh 500 |

If any number drifts outside these, that's not "a bad week" - it's a signal to
change something.

---

## 14. One-Page Start

If you do nothing else, keep 3 numbers updated every single day:
1. **Cash balance** (M-Pesa + bank today).
2. **Units sold today** and revenue.
3. **Stock bought / money owed** today.

That single habit keeps a small store alive through everything else you learn
later.
